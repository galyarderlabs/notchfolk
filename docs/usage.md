# Using Notchfolk

This guide covers everyday use, the settings, troubleshooting and uninstalling. For installing, see the [installation guide](install.md); for the README, [go up](../README.md).

## Usage

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Rest the pointer on the notch | Open the island (or click it, if Settings say so) |
| Click a tab, or scroll or swipe over the tabs | Switch pages (scrolling a page's own content never does) |
| Move the pointer away, or press Esc | Close it |
| Click the notch | Open it now; on an alert, open what it is about |
| Middle-click the notch | Play or pause |
| Rest the pointer on an alert | Keep it until the pointer leaves |
| Swipe up or down on an alert (touchpad) | Put it away, or open it |
| Right-click the notch | Put an alert away; else bring the call, or the music player, to the front |
| Super+V | Open the clipboard history with search ready; Enter pastes |
| Drag a file towards the notch | Open the files tray to drop it |
| Super+I | Ask the voice assistant; press again to finish speaking, or after an answer to ask more. Start typing (or click the keyboard) to type the request instead |
| Enter or Esc on the assistant's card | Run or Deny the command it shows |

The island reopens on the page you picked last (for five minutes), or on
what is live: a call, unseen notifications, music, a running timer. With two
music players running, the music page shows the other one's icon to switch
to it.

While a window is fullscreen (a video, a game), the island stays out of the
way: alerts, notifications and reminders wait and show once it is gone, and
a burst of notifications comes as one ("6 new notifications"), each still
unread in the list. A reminder stays until you are back at the computer.
While you share or record the screen, alerts leave out what notifications
say, and the clipboard history stays hidden. If Notchfolk's background service
stops, a warning shows in the closed island; click it to start it again.

### What each page does

The gestures in the table above work everywhere. What each page does, in the same order as the Features in the README:

**Music.**
Your music in the island — cover, scrubber and controls.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Play from Spotify, YouTube in your browser, or any MPRIS player | Cover, scrubber and controls, with the playing app's icon on the cover |
| Click the other player's icon | Switch players, with two music players running |

**Synced lyrics.**
The line being sung, large and bright.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Turn on lyrics online | Lyrics load from LRCLIB while songs play |
| Click a line | Jump to it |
| Play a song LRCLIB does not know | Shows "No lyrics found" |

**Calls.**
Your calls in the island.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Take a call in WhatsApp, Telegram, Zoom, Meet or a browser call page | A green phone in the closed island, the call's length on the Call page |
| Open the call | Watch it live while you work elsewhere, mute, or jump back to the call |

**Timer.**
A Pomodoro timer with breaks and Do Not Disturb while you focus.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Type or step with − and + | Set the work and break lengths (locked from Start until Reset) |
| Press Start | The timer runs; Do Not Disturb stays on while you focus |
| Glance at the closed island | The time left stays there; beside a song or a call it shows as a small ring |

**Reminders.**
Tasks that clear themselves at midnight.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Add a task | It joins the list, with the number still open beside it |
| Rename in place | Change a task's title |
| Tick a task | Finish it — done ones clear at midnight |

**Installs and fixes things.**
Ask it to install or fix something on your Linux.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Say "Install LibreOffice" or "pasang codex" | It checks what your Linux has, searches the web when unsure, and shows the one command it wants to run, with what to know first |
| Press Run | It runs in the background while you carry on |
| Press Deny | It does not run |
| Wait for the answer | The assistant checks it worked before saying so |

**Weather.**
Your city's weather and the week ahead.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Choose a city in Settings, or follow the GNOME World Clock | The forecast for that city, or the one nearest your time zone |
| Set the temperature's unit | Auto, Celsius or Fahrenheit |
| Open the page | Current conditions, humidity, wind, sunrise and sunset, an hourly curve colored by each forecast condition, and the five-day forecast |
| Look for earthquakes | Nearby ones from BMKG in Indonesia and USGS elsewhere; USGS fills in when BMKG has no nearby recent event |

**Calendar.**
The month beside the chosen day's events.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Connect your desktop calendars | Google and others through Online Accounts |
| Type "Dinner 8:00 pm" | Add an event in one line |
| Wait 5 minutes before | A reminder, with Join for Meet, Zoom, Teams or Webex |
| Look underneath | World clocks |

**Notifications.**
GNOME's notifications as a plain list.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Open the list | Newest first, with their buttons under the pointer |
| Press Clear | Clear the list |

**Voice assistant.**
Press Super+I and ask, in English or Indonesian.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Press Super+I again | Finish speaking, or after an answer, ask more |
| Ask for something with several steps | It is done one step after another, each step checked |
| Pick the AI in Settings | Claude Code, Codex, Grok, Hermes, Oh My Pi, opencode or Antigravity answers |

**Shelf.**
Clipboard, files and your phone, in the island.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Search, filter, pin | Find text, links, images and files you copied |
| Drag a file out of the tray, or click it | Drop it into any app, or copy it |
| Scan the QR | Save shelf files on your phone or send files back, in the browser, same Wi-Fi, nothing through the internet |
| Ring phone, Send clipboard | Reach your GSConnect phone from the Devices image |

**Speak or type, and talk it through.**
The conversation is the chat itself.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Type in Ask anything at the bottom | Keep talking |
| Pick a past conversation | Reopen it, with its message count beside it |
| Press + New chat | Start a new one |

**A bot of your own.**
Eight shapes, eleven colours, one glow to match.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Pick a shape in Settings | From a circle to a cloud or a ghost |
| Pick a colour, or your own | The glow round the island matches it |
| Open the live preview | See the bot before you pick it |

**AI usage.**
How much of your Claude and ChatGPT plan limits is used.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Open the page | One plan at a time with its logo, a bar per limit and when each resets |
| Scroll or click the dots | Switch plans |
| Choose plans in Settings | Only those plans show; only plans you're signed in to show at all |
| Watch for the warning | One comes as a limit nears its end |

**Alerts.**
Everything that needs you, in one place instead of the middle of the screen.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Wait for an alert | Volume, brightness, Caps Lock, charging, low battery, Bluetooth battery, going offline, reminders, meetings about to start, AI plan limits and GNOME notifications with their buttons |
| Hold the pointer on one | Keep it |
| Press × or swipe up | Put it away |
| Switch each alert on or off in Settings | Only those alerts show |

**AI agents.**
Answer agent permission requests from the island.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Press Deny | It does not run |
| Press Allow | It runs once |
| Press Always (Claude Code) | Always allow it |
| Wait for the island | When one is done, it says what it did |
| Switch an agent off in Settings | Its approvals stop showing |

**App usage.**
Where your time goes.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Turn it on | Tracking starts — off until you turn it on |
| Pick today, the last seven days, or the last 30 | The app in front then, with its icon, a bar and its time |
| Leave the computer idle for five minutes | Tracking pauses; locking the screen stops it |
| Look back later | Kept on this computer for 30 days |

**Glass.**
The island in clear dark glass instead of black, if you prefer.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Choose Glass in Settings → Appearance → Island | What is behind it shows through, blurred, as you work |
| Move the slider | From clear to dark smoked glass |

**Vitals.**
The machine's own pulse in the island.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Open the page | CPU load and temperature big with the last minute drawn as a sparkline between them, each core a bar, the load averages and the process count, memory / swap / disk bars with their bytes, the network's rates, the battery and the uptime |
| Wait 2 s | Fresh numbers, sampled from /proc, no extension needed |
| Pick a vital in Settings | It rides the closed island beside the wave |
| Set the temperature's unit | Auto, Celsius or Fahrenheit |

**Karaoke.**
The line being sung, in the closed island.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Play a song with synced lyrics | The closed island's centre becomes the line being sung — the cover left, the wave right, the clock waiting |
| Sing along | The line holds one width for the whole song, so the island never pumps; a short line centres in the room its last long line held |

**Router.**
What your AI gateway reports.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Connect | Requests, tokens in and out, its cost estimate, the top models as bars, and the last ten requests — a health dot once connected |
| Set the period | Last 7 days, or your choice |
| Turn on Show on the notch | Router stats ride the closed island |

**Everyday tools.**
Water, calories and prayer times.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Log water and calories through the day | Today's totals, kept on this computer |
| Open the Prayer page | The day's prayer times on a sun arc, with the next one and how long until it |

**MCP server.**
An optional MCP server for agents outside Notchfolk.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| List writes in <code>~/.config/notchfolk/mcp.env</code> | Those writes are allowed; read tools work for your data and actions |
| Give a key no scope | Its restricted tools are not even visible to it |
| Open remote access | Reach it through an authenticated tunnel; on loopback by default |
| Look at the world domains | Seven, 150 tools in all: System (the machine itself — stats, logs, files, processes, updates, the sharp tools behind their own scope), Browser (the web through a real browser: Camoufox, or your own Chromium over CDP), Memory (the Hindsight engine's 39 tools — what the agent learns, kept across conversations), Notes (your own vault of Markdown notes, and memory's fallback), Skills (the world knows how to do things: the skill trees on the machine), Office (the world makes documents) and Composio (the world reaches your accounts — mail and drive, read on the default key, writes behind their own scope) |
| Run without a backend | A domain whose backend is absent is not offered at all |

**More AIs.**
More AIs for the assistant.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Pick a provider in Settings | The assistant runs Hermes and Oh My Pi with the providers they are configured for |
| Choose OpenAI-compatible API | Any OpenAI-compatible API answers |

## Settings

Click the gear right of the clock in the open island (or Notchfolk's gear in the
**Extensions** app):

- **Island:** Notchfolk's own black island, or Glass, clear dark glass that shows what
  is behind it (windows and wallpaper) blurred, with a slider from clear to dark smoked glass; the top bar on a slider from glass (default) to black, and black anyway while a window reaches it (off by default);
  a little space below the island inside the bar (on by default); auto-hide (always, with a top bar that hides, or beside maximized windows; off by default); a 24- or 12-hour clock (or as GNOME's); privacy dots; which pages the open island has (Music,
  Notifications, Timer, Reminders, Shelf, Calendar, Apps, AI Usage, Vitals) and whether it
  has the AI assistant at all; world clocks, and their cities;
  the closed island inside the bar or hanging below it; with more than one
  display, on the main one or the one you are using; opening when the
  pointer rests on it (and for how long) or only on a click; how long auto-hide waits.
- **Alerts:** apps' notifications in the island (or GNOME's own banners
  instead); over full-screen apps too (off by default); volume and brightness; charging, network, Bluetooth and Caps
  Lock, each on its own; new songs, sounds; lyrics online;
  a reminder before calendar events, and how many minutes before.
  Alerts other than a low battery are not shown while Do Not Disturb is on.
- **Calls:** show calls in the island, and the live preview of the call.
- **Weather:** the city, or following the GNOME World Clock nearest your time
  zone; the temperature's unit — auto, Celsius or Fahrenheit.
- **Clipboard:** turn the history on or off, clear it, what Super+V opens (the
  clipboard history, or GNOME's calendar), and choose apps whose copies are never
  kept (password managers are on the list from the start).
- **Shelf:** how many files the files tray holds — as many as you like; a long
  row scrolls.
- **Timer:** session and break lengths, Do Not Disturb while focusing.
- **Vitals:** the page on or off, which vital rides the closed island beside the
  wave (none by default), and the temperature's unit — auto (the locale's own),
  Celsius or Fahrenheit.
- **Assistant:** the bot's look (shape, colour, glow, with a live
  preview); the assistant on or off, its shortcut, which AI answers and with
  which model (only those your plan includes, with your plan's name), speech recognition, language, and whether it may use the text
  you selected (off by default) or read your notifications; how long a quiet
  conversation is kept, and clearing it now.
- **AI Usage:** on or off, which plans to show, and when to warn; and
  under **AI Agents**, a switch for each of Claude Code and Codex that is
  installed (on by default; none listed when neither is).
- **About:** the version and whether it is up to date (with how to update,
  and the switch for asking GitHub once a day), help, a bug report to copy
  (`notchfolkd --report`), and resetting every setting.

## Troubleshooting

**The island does not appear after installing.** Log out and back in (on
Wayland this is required once). Then check that it is enabled:
`gnome-extensions info io.galyarderlabs.notchfolk`.

**Music or lyrics do not show.** Check that the background service runs:
`systemctl --user status notchfolkd`, and read its log with
`journalctl --user -u notchfolkd -f`. Songs LRCLIB does not know show "No lyrics
found"; a busy service retries on its own.

**Files cannot be dragged in or out.** The release package includes the
drag-and-drop helper; when building from source, install `libgtk-4-dev`
(Fedora: `gtk4-devel`) and run `./install.sh` again.

**A phone can't open the Shelf.** It must be on the same Wi-Fi as the computer
(not a guest network that keeps devices apart). A firewall that blocks
incoming connections keeps it out too: with ufw, the island offers the
command (**Copy fix**), `sudo ufw allow from <your network> to any port 47830:47839 proto tcp`;
with firewalld on a zone that blocks them (Fedora Workstation's own does not),
`sudo firewall-cmd --permanent --add-port=47830-47839/tcp && sudo firewall-cmd --reload`.
Notchfolk never changes the firewall itself, and listens only while you share.

**Another extension covers the island.** Extensions that restyle or move the
top bar (Dash to Panel, Hide Top Bar, other islands) can get in the way;
turn them off in the Extensions app.

**The voice assistant does not answer.** Run `~/.local/bin/notchfolkd --doctor`: it says what
is missing (speech recognition, an AI, or its sign-in) and what to run.

**Something else.** Run `~/.local/bin/notchfolkd --report` (or Settings → About → Copy a bug
report): it gathers the versions, what works and recent logs, without your
data. Please [open an issue](../../issues) and paste it there.

With a .deb, .rpm or AUR package, Notchfolk's commands are in `/usr/bin`: run
them by name alone (`notchfolkd --doctor`, `notchfolkd --report`).

## Uninstalling

```sh
~/.local/bin/notchfolk-uninstall
```

(or `./uninstall.sh` from the release package or the source folder). This
removes the extension, the programs, the service, the font, the voice
assistant's speech recognition and Notchfolk's caches, and lifts a battery charge
limit Notchfolk set; it also takes Notchfolk's hooks out of Claude Code's and Codex's
settings. Your tasks, calendar events, clipboard history, shelf and
timer settings stay in `~/.local/share/notchfolk` until you delete them.

Remove Notchfolk this way, not with the Extensions app's Remove button, which
deletes only the extension and leaves `notchfolkd` and its hooks behind. For a
system-wide package, each user runs `notchfolkd --unhook-all` first (and
`notchfolk-charge-limit 100` if the assistant set a battery charge limit), then
remove it with `sudo apt remove notchfolk`, `sudo dnf remove notchfolk` or
`sudo pacman -R notchfolk-bin`.
